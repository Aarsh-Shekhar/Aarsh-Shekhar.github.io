Travel May 2026 4 min read

Villages, airports, and the strange privilege of being changed by a place.

Traveling has made the world feel less like a map and more like a set of rooms I have been invited to enter carefully. The moments that stay with me are rarely the obvious ones. I remember small villages where people repaired things instead of replacing them, meals that stretched past language, roads that made every errand feel like a collaboration with weather, distance, and chance.

The most profound part was realizing how narrow my default assumptions can be. In places where the rhythm of life is slower, harder, or simply different, intelligence does not always look like a resume or a product launch. Sometimes it looks like knowing exactly when the rain will turn, how to make a machine last ten more years, or how to treat a stranger with dignity before asking where they are from.

I came back less interested in collecting countries and more interested in becoming the kind of person who can pay attention.

That has changed how I think about engineering too. Technology should not flatten difference into one clean interface and call it progress. The best systems, like the best travelers, begin by listening.

College June 2026 5 min read

Why I chose UT Austin ECE.

Choosing UT Austin for ECE was not the simple version of the story. I was lucky enough to have options that would have sounded impossible to me a few years ago: Berkeley, Penn, Duke, Cornell, and other places with names that carry real weight. For a while, I kept trying to solve the decision like an optimization problem. Rankings, networks, labs, cities, cost, prestige, weather, probability of regret. The spreadsheet got longer. The answer did not get much clearer.

What eventually mattered was fit. UT ECE felt close to the kind of builder I want to become: technical enough to stretch me, large enough to contain many versions of ambition, and grounded in a city where hardware, software, research, startups, and public life keep bumping into each other. It also felt like a place where I could be excellent without performing a borrowed personality.

There is a weird pressure to choose the option that sounds most impressive in one sentence. I understand that pressure. I felt it. But I am trying to make decisions that still make sense after the announcement is over, when the work begins and nobody is applauding the acceptance letter anymore.

UT Austin was not me choosing against other schools. It was me choosing toward the life I could see myself actually building.

Engineering July 2026 4 min read

On building things that can survive contact with real people.

A lot of student projects are built for demos. I have built some of those. The screen works, the pitch lands, the judges nod, and then the whole thing quietly becomes a folder with a good name. I do not think that is failure. Demos are how we learn to make ideas visible. But lately I have been more interested in the next question: what would it take for this to survive?

Survival changes the design. You start caring about boring things that are not boring at all: latency, unclear inputs, tired users, privacy, bad Wi-Fi, edge cases, trust. A real system has to make room for people who do not read the instructions and for days when its creator is not standing nearby to explain why the bug is actually a feature.

That is the kind of engineering I want to practice more. Not just cleverness, though cleverness is fun. Not just ambition, though ambition matters. I want to build with enough humility that the thing can be questioned, repaired, and improved by reality.

The best projects do not only prove that you can make something work. They teach you what working should mean.
